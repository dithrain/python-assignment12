Static visualizations are best for showing a snapshot of data, but they don’t change once they're made. 
They're and easy to share, but if someone wants to dig deeper, they have to make a new chart. 
Interactive visualizations let people explore the data themselves by zooming, filtering, 
or hovering to see details. This makes them more flexible and useful when the audience has different questions
 about the same dataset.

Dashboards put multiple charts and controls together in one place. They allow real-time data exploration, so 
instead of flipping through slides or reports, someone can just change filters to change results. This makes them
 more practical if the data changes often.

Plotly and Dash improve data communication by making complex information easier to understand. Instead 
of just sending numbers or images, an interactive chart lets the audience connect with the data to their preference. 
In a professional setting, this helps folks from technical and non-technical backgrounds get insights faster, 
which can improve teamwork and and make decision making easier.
